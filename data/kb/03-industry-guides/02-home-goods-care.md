## Categories covered

Ceramics & glass, hand-blown glassware, table linens, bedding, rugs and textiles, wood furniture, upholstery, candles & home fragrance, kitchen knives, cookware.

## Ceramics & glass

**Hand-thrown ceramics** vary slightly piece to piece. Variations are not defects — they are a record of the throwing. Care:

- Hand wash for everyday pieces with a complex glaze. Dishwasher-safe glazes are noted on the product page; assume hand wash if the page doesn't say.
- Avoid sudden temperature changes (refrigerator to oven, freezer to stovetop). Most ceramics tolerate gradients but not shocks.
- A fine craze or crackle in a glaze (intentional, often called crackle glaze) is decorative and stable. A stress crack that has formed since purchase is a defect — contact concierge.

**Hand-blown glass** has trapped bubbles, occasional asymmetry, and individuality of weight. None are defects. Hand wash with a soft sponge; never the dishwasher rack. Avoid stacking.

## Table linens & bedding

**Linen** softens with every wash for the first 50 washes, then plateaus. Wash cool, line dry or low tumble, iron damp if you want crispness. Linen with horsehair embroidery: hand wash only.

**Cotton percale** runs cool; cotton sateen runs warm. Either takes high-heat washing well. Iron from the back of the print.

**Hemp blends** stiffen if over-dried. Always pull from the dryer slightly damp.

A good practice: rotate two sheet sets per bed. Doubles their lifespan.

## Rugs

**Wool rugs** shed for the first 90 days. This is normal. Vacuum gently in the direction of the pile. Do not use a beater bar on hand-knotted pieces. Rotate 180° every 6 months for even wear.

**Flatweave (kilim, dhurrie, durry)** can be vacuumed in either direction. Spot clean with cool water and a colorfast-tested cloth.

**Spills** — blot, never rub. For wine, soda water and patience. For oil, dry-clean only.

## Wood furniture

Most of our wood pieces are finished with hard-wax oil. They develop a patina. Care:

- Dust with a soft, dry cloth. No furniture polish, no Pledge, no silicone.
- Re-oil annually using the maker's recommended oil. Each piece ships with the right product or a link to it.
- Use felt pads under everything that sits on the surface for more than a meal.
- Do not let standing water sit. Coasters, place mats, basic care.

Solid-wood pieces will move with humidity. Small seasonal gaps in a panel are not defects. They will close again in the opposite season.

## Upholstery

**Loose-weave fabrics** require gentle vacuuming and prompt attention to spills. Most of our upholstery is treated against minor stains; it is not magic. Always blot.

**Leather upholstery** — see fashion guide for leather. Same rules apply.

**Cushion rotation** — rotate seat cushions weekly during heavy use to even out compression.

## Candles & fragrance

**First burn** — let the wax pool reach the edge of the vessel. This sets the burn pattern. Skipping this step causes tunneling.

**Trim wicks to 5mm** before each burn. Trims reduce mushrooming, soot, and uneven flame.

**Maximum burn 4 hours.** Past four hours, burn quality degrades and the vessel can over-heat.

**Re-use the vessel.** When the wax is gone, freeze the candle, pop out the residual disk, wash the vessel. Most of our candle vessels become elegant small storage.

## Kitchen knives & cookware

**Hand wash carbon steel and high-carbon kitchen knives.** Dry immediately. They will rust if left wet. A thin film of mineral oil on the blade extends life.

**Cast iron** — wipe clean with a paper towel, salt-scrub if needed, dry over heat, oil lightly. Soap is fine on a seasoned surface despite folklore.

**Copper cookware** — most of ours is tin-lined or stainless-lined. Hand wash; no abrasive scrubbers. Polish the exterior occasionally for shine, or let it patina to the natural mid-brown of well-used copper.

**Hand-thrown stoneware bakeware** — preheat with the oven; never put cold stoneware into a hot oven.

## When something goes wrong

A defect, a finish failure, a structural issue — see *Damaged or Defective Items*. Most home items are covered by our 24-month or 5-year Care Promise (textiles vs. furniture). For everyday wear-and-tear, our partner restorers handle most categories.
