## Why fashion care matters more here

Most of our garments are made in small runs, often in fabrics that behave differently from mass-market equivalents. A heavy raw silk does not wash like a polyester crepe. A vegetable-tanned leather will mark in ways a chrome-tanned leather will not. The price you paid reflected the textile and the labor; the way you care for it should reflect the same considerations.

We publish care guidance on every product page. This document is the master reference behind it.

## Washing

**Cold over warm. Always.** We default to cold water washing for almost everything except whites and underwear. Cold preserves dyes, especially plant-based ones, and causes less fiber breakdown.

**Inside-out for prints, embroidery, sequins, anything fragile.** Reduces friction against other garments and the drum.

**Mesh bags for small items and delicate knits.** Prevents tangling and stretching.

**Liquid detergent over powder for our garments.** Powder doesn't always fully dissolve in cold water and can leave residues on dark fabrics.

**Avoid optical brighteners** for any natural-dyed or undyed fabric. Read the back of the bottle. Brighteners interfere with the depth and patina that natural fibers develop.

## Drying

**Lay flat for knitwear.** Always. Hanging a wet sweater is the most common reason a knit becomes a pear shape.

**Line dry for linen, raw silk, hand-dyed cotton.** Direct sunlight is fine for most natural fibers and helps with mild brightening; avoid sustained direct sun for naturally-dyed pieces, which will fade.

**Tumble dry only what the label permits.** When in doubt, do not. Heat shrinks, felts, and weakens.

## Ironing & steaming

A handheld steamer is the single best fashion-care purchase a customer can make. It removes 80% of the wrinkles a garment will accumulate, with no risk of scorch.

For cotton shirting and linen suits, an iron at the right temperature is still the gold standard. The temperature settings published on most modern irons are calibrated to fiber: cotton high, linen high, silk medium, wool low, synthetics low.

## Storage

**Knitwear: folded.** Hangers will distort the shoulders over time.

**Tailoring: hung on a contoured hanger.** Wire hangers will deform a shoulder line within months. Hold suits on shaped wooden or thick contour hangers.

**Off-season: breathable garment bags, not plastic.** Plastic traps moisture and accelerates moth interest.

**Cedar over mothballs.** Cedar does the job and your closet does not smell like mothballs.

## Leather

**Vegetable-tanned leather** changes character over years. It will darken, soften, and develop a patina. Mild conditioning every 6 months with a wax-free conditioner. Do not over-condition; over-condition is more common than under-condition.

**Chrome-tanned leather** is more stable and requires less attention. Wipe with a slightly damp cloth, let dry, condition once a year.

**Suede and nubuck** require a brush, never a damp cloth. A waterproofing spray applied before first wear extends life considerably.

For all leather: keep away from direct sunlight, radiators, and rain. If a leather piece gets wet, let it air-dry naturally (no hairdryer) and condition once dry.
