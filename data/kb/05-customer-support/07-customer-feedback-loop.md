# Customer Feedback Loop

**Document type:** Internal — operations
**Owner:** Saket Panwar
**Last reviewed:** 02 March 2026

---

## What the loop is for

The loop exists to close the gap between what customers tell us and what changes inside the company. Feedback that does not produce change is performative. We have a finite tolerance for performative work.

The loop has four stages: capture, route, decide, communicate.

## Capture

We capture customer feedback from seven sources:

1. **Direct support tickets** — every concierge interaction is tagged.
2. **Post-purchase satisfaction (CSAT)** — sent 7 days after delivery.
3. **NPS** — sent annually to all customers, semi-annually to Circle members.
4. **Returns reasons** — required field on the return form.
5. **Social listening** — passive monitoring of mentions.
6. **Brand-relayed** — feedback the brand received and forwarded.
7. **Founder office hours** — Saket holds a monthly open-Zoom hour for any customer who wants to talk; ~12-18 attendees per session.

We do not run pop-up surveys on the website. We do not chase customers for review submissions.

## Route

Every piece of feedback gets a route. Feedback is tagged with:

- **Type** — product issue, service issue, feature request, editorial feedback, brand-specific, sustainability concern, accessibility concern, pricing concern.
- **Severity** — single-incident, emerging pattern, established pattern.
- **Owner team** — concierge, brand operations, product, editorial, logistics, sustainability.

Feedback flows into a shared workspace visible to leadership. Each tag has a default owner. Each owner is responsible for triage within 5 business days of receipt.

## Decide

Owners categorize feedback into four buckets:

**Fix immediately.** Specific service errors, clear bugs, individual customer harm. Action within 24-72 hours.

**Fix in next planning cycle.** Patterns that need design or engineering work. Goes into the relevant team's queue. Customer is told the change is coming.

**Considered, not changing.** Sometimes the right answer is no. We say so, with reasoning. We do not tell the customer "we'll consider it" if we have already decided no.

**Need more signal.** Single data point insufficient to act on. We watch for repeats.

The four-bucket discipline is non-negotiable. We do not let "we'll consider it" become a black hole.

## Communicate

When a piece of feedback produces change, we communicate it:

- **Personally** — the originating customer is told that their feedback drove the change. A short, real message from the person who took the action.
- **Publicly** — significant changes are noted in our quarterly journal and on the product update page.

When a piece of feedback does NOT produce change, we still communicate. The originating customer gets a brief, honest note: "We thought hard about this. Here's why we're not changing it." This is harder to write than the easy yes; it's also more important.

## Examples from the last year

A few real(ish) cases from our internal review:

**Customer feedback:** "The product page font is too small on iPhone."
**Outcome:** Engineering bumped the body font from 14px to 16px on mobile after the third comment. We confirmed back to all three customers.

**Customer feedback:** "Why don't you sell to Iceland?"
**Outcome:** We added Iceland to our shipping markets. Iceland-based customer who first asked got a hand-delivered note when our shipping lane went live.

**Customer feedback:** "Your candles arrive smelling weak."
**Outcome:** Investigated with the brand. Determined: customer was burning new candles at room temperature in a draft. Sent customer a guidance note and a small gift candle. No platform change.

**Customer feedback:** "You should sell electronics."
**Outcome:** No. Replied with a thanks-but-no, explaining that our curation depth in electronics couldn't match what we hold elsewhere.

**Customer feedback:** "Why doesn't the search find things by mood?"
**Outcome:** This is now a major project, due Q3 2026. The customer who first asked is on the beta list.

## Things we are wary of

**The vocal minority bias.** Loud feedback is not always representative feedback. We weight by data signals, not by message volume.

**The loyal customer trap.** Long-term customers are often resistant to change that benefits new customers. We listen, but we do not let backwards-compatibility become a default.

**The "I'd buy more if…" objection.** We hear this from people who would, on inspection, not. We weight against it gently.

**The weighted-survey trap.** Surveys with leading questions produce comforting answers. We rotate authors and audit instruments quarterly.

## Reporting

Quarterly: a written feedback report to the executive team and brand partner network. Includes themes, actions taken, actions deferred, themes still under investigation, surprises.

Annually: a public-facing customer letter from Saket summarizing the year in feedback. Honest about what didn't change, and why.
