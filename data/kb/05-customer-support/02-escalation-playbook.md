# Escalation Playbook

**Document type:** Internal — concierge & operations
**Owner:** Saket Panwar
**Last reviewed:** 12 February 2026
**Audience:** All customer-facing teammates.

---

## When to escalate

Escalate when the situation crosses any of the following:

1. **Health, safety, or regulatory.** Any reported injury, allergic reaction, contamination, choking risk, suspected stolen-goods listing, suspected fraud, regulatory inquiry.
2. **Press, legal, or social media.** A customer mentions journalism, social broadcasting, the press team, or legal action. (We don't escalate because they're posting — we escalate because the situation warrants attention.)
3. **Material policy exception.** A request that would require deviating from a written customer policy beyond your normal authority (e.g., a $300 goodwill credit, a return outside the 60-day window for a non-Care-Promise reason).
4. **Brand-side serious issue.** A pattern emerging at a single brand: 3+ damage reports in 7 days, multiple authenticity questions, multiple sizing complaints on a single SKU.
5. **Unfamiliar or complex.** You don't know what the right answer is. This is a normal reason to escalate. Do not guess.

## What to do *before* escalating

Two minutes of work that often resolves the situation:

- Re-read the customer's message slowly. The right answer is often there.
- Pull the order, the product page, the brand's metadata, and the customer's account history.
- Check the relevant policy doc and any internal reference for this exact situation.
- Decide what *you* think the right answer is. Don't escalate empty-handed.

## How to escalate

Use the *Escalate* button in the support workspace. The button creates a ticket in the escalation queue, notifies the on-shift escalations lead, and posts to *#cs-escalations* with severity flag.

In the escalation note, include:

- Customer name + order number.
- One-paragraph summary in plain language.
- Your proposed resolution.
- Any specific time pressure ("customer is leaving on a flight at 4pm").
- Why you escalated.

Do NOT escalate by silence ("just transferring you to my supervisor"). Do NOT escalate without proposing a resolution.

## Authority levels

Concierge associates can resolve up to:

- $200 USD goodwill credit per case.
- Return outside policy if within Care Promise.
- Reship without return for items under $200 USD.
- Expedited shipping upgrade.

Senior concierge can resolve up to:

- $500 USD goodwill credit.
- Return outside policy on case-by-case judgment.
- Free Care Promise extension by up to 6 months.
- Brand-side investigation request.

Escalations lead can resolve:

- Above-$500 credits.
- Custom resolutions involving sourcing, replacement, or commission.
- Brand-side delisting recommendations.
- Press / legal coordination.

VP Customer Operations and General Counsel handle the rest.

## Severity flags

| Flag | What it means | Response time |
|---|---|---|
| Sev-1 | Health, safety, regulatory, legal, press | < 30 minutes |
| Sev-2 | Material business risk; multi-customer pattern | < 2 hours |
| Sev-3 | Standard escalation | < 4 business hours |
| Sev-4 | FYI, no action needed | < 24 hours |

## Communicating with the customer during escalation

The customer should not feel passed-around. Tell them clearly:

- "I'm bringing in a colleague who can do something I can't."
- An estimated time to next contact, conservative.
- Your name; the new owner's name when they take over.

If you cannot promise an answer in the next two hours, say so. Do not over-promise.

## After resolution

Every Sev-1 and Sev-2 escalation generates a brief written post-mortem written by the resolution owner within 48 hours. The post-mortem covers what happened, what we did, what we'd do differently. Post-mortems are read at our weekly operations meeting and shared back to the originating team.

Post-mortems are not blame documents. We do not name individuals in any negative framing. We do recognize good handling.

## Common patterns

A few patterns that come up often:

**The "I'm not asking for a refund — I just want you to know" customer.** Slow down. They are giving us a gift: feedback without a transactional ask. Acknowledge, share with the brand, and follow up with what changed.

**The 8th-time-this-month customer with a small complaint.** Pull the history. There may be a real fit issue, a delivery zone issue, or an account compromise we should investigate. Do not assume abuse.

**The high-value customer with an unreasonable demand.** Lifetime value is real but it does not buy unlimited concession. The right answer is the right answer. Document carefully.

**The "I work at X press outlet" customer.** Treat the case on its merits. Do not give better service because they're press. Do not give worse service. Same standard.
