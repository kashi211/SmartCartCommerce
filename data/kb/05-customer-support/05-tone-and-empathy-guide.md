# Support Tone & Empathy Guide

**Document type:** Internal — concierge
**Owner:** Saket Panwar
**Last reviewed:** 22 February 2026

---

## The starting point

Every SmartCartCommerce support response starts from the same place: the person on the other side is having a less good day than they hoped to have. Sometimes that's because of us. Sometimes it isn't. In either case, our job is to remove the smallest amount of friction we can in the most considered way we can.

The voice in *Brand Voice & Tone Guidelines* is the voice. This document is about how to apply it in support specifically — where speed and care have to coexist.

## Five things to do

### 1. Read carefully, then read again

Most support failures begin with a misread first message. Read slowly. Reread once. The second pass surfaces the actual concern about 30% of the time.

### 2. Acknowledge the substance, not just the feeling

"That's frustrating!" is empty. "Three weeks is a long time to wait for a kettle, especially when the listing said two" — that lands, because it shows you read.

### 3. Lead with the resolution

The customer wants to know what happens next. Lead with the answer. Explanations come after, briefly.

**Do:** "Refund issued; you'll see it in 3-5 business days. Here's what happened on our end…"

**Don't:** "After investigating with our logistics team, taking into account the carrier's reporting, etc., etc., we have decided… [refund]"

### 4. Use the customer's name

Once at the start of the message. Once at the end if it lands naturally. Not in every paragraph.

### 5. Sign with your name

Not "the SmartCartCommerce team." Not "your concierge." Your actual name. We are real people, and being a real person is part of the service.

## Five things not to do

### 1. Over-apologize

One clear apology is more powerful than five. "I'm sorry — that's on us." Done. Now move to the resolution.

### 2. Hide behind policy

If the answer is policy, we say so plainly: "Our return window is 60 days, and you're at day 73 — usually I'd default to no, but the situation here is sympathetic, so let me make an exception." The phrase "per our policy" is banned.

### 3. Use customer-service jargon

Banned phrases: "Per our records." "We regret to inform you." "Unfortunately, we are unable to." "As a one-time courtesy." "We appreciate your patience." "Have a great day!" None of these belong in our voice.

### 4. Send a templated reply when the situation isn't templated

Templates are useful for the obvious — a return label send, a tracking lookup. They are wrong for anything emotionally nuanced. If the customer wrote three paragraphs, a one-line auto-response is an insult.

### 5. End on an upsell

Don't pivot from "your $300 sweater arrived with a hole" to "by the way, our new arrivals…" The mood is wrong. Resolve the issue. Land the apology. Stop.

## Specific situations

### When we made a mistake

Own it directly. No hedging, no euphemism, no "I understand how this could have been frustrating." The customer wants to know we know.

> Hi (customer) — yes, that was on us. I checked the dispatch log; we shipped the wrong size. Replacement is going out today on express; you'll have it Wednesday. Keep the wrong one for now or send it back in the prepaid pouch — whichever's easier. Sorry for the wobble.

### When the customer is wrong (and it matters)

Be direct, but kind. Don't pretend they were right. Don't pretend they were wrong-but-we'll-fix-it-anyway when we won't.

> Hi — I want to be straight with you. The discount code expired the week before you used it, which is why it didn't apply. I can't retroactively apply it, but I can offer you free express shipping on this order if you'd like to keep it. Let me know.

### When the customer is upset and unreasonable

Stay calm. Stay short. Acknowledge the feeling once. Repeat the answer. Do not match the energy.

If the customer is abusive (insults, slurs, threats), end the conversation:

> I want to help, but I'm not able to continue this exchange while it's being directed at me. I'll close the ticket here and a senior teammate will reach out within 24 hours. Take care.

### When the customer is going through something hard

A bereavement, a job loss, a divorce, an illness. The right response is brief, human, not over-warm. Resolve the practical thing they asked about. Add one sentence acknowledging the human moment. Stop.

> Hi — I've cancelled and refunded the gift order. No paperwork on your end. I'm so sorry about your father.

### When the customer is happy

Yes, this happens. Receive it well. A short thank-you is enough. Do not respond with a coupon. Do not pivot to a request for a review. Let the moment be what it is.

## Length

Most replies are 3-6 sentences. Long replies are reserved for situations that need explanation. If you're writing more than three paragraphs, ask yourself why. The answer is usually that you should be picking up the phone (Circle) or escalating (anyone).

## Voice modulation

Match the customer's register without aping it. If they wrote casually, write casually. If they wrote formally, write formally. Don't go past where they are. A formal customer should not be answered with "Heyyy."

## Channel notes

- **Email** — most communication. Subject lines descriptive ("Re: refund issued — order #L82041") not generic.
- **Live chat** — shorter, more contractions, faster.
- **Phone (Circle only)** — voice. Read tone of voice. Confirm action items in writing afterward.
- **Social DMs** — public-adjacent. Be especially careful. Move to email or chat for anything beyond a quick question.
