# VIP & High-Value Customer Handling

**Document type:** Internal — concierge
**Owner:** Saket Panwar
**Last reviewed:** 28 February 2026

---

## What we mean by VIP

We do not use the term "VIP" with customers. Internally it's a useful shorthand for three overlapping populations who get extra care:

1. **SmartCart Circle members** — paid annual membership; named concierge.
2. **High-lifetime-value customers** — top 1% by trailing 24-month spend, regardless of Circle status.
3. **Press, gifting partners, and brand founders' households** — flagged manually.

Roughly 1.6% of our active customer base falls into one or more of these categories.

## What changes for VIPs

The substance of our service does not change. Everyone gets the same policies, the same product quality, the same honest answers. What changes is *speed*, *attention*, and *small unprompted gestures*.

| Aspect | Standard | VIP |
|---|---|---|
| Response time | < 90 minutes business hours | < 30 minutes business hours |
| First contact | Routed to next available | Routed to named concierge (Circle) or experienced senior (others) |
| Goodwill threshold | Per standard authority | Senior authority by default |
| Unprompted check-in | After significant issues | After any issue, even resolved ones |
| Restock alerts | Via newsletter | Personal note from concierge for items they viewed/saved |
| Returns | Same policy | Pickup arranged; no label printing required |

## What does NOT change

- Policy. VIPs do not get policy exceptions just for being VIPs. They get an exception when an exception is the right answer for the situation, regardless of who they are.
- Honesty. We do not soften bad news. Wealthy customers tend to detect this and dislike it.
- Pricing. No discounts. No "thanks for being a Circle member" markdowns. The relationship is the value.
- Quality. We do not pull a higher-grade item from inventory for a VIP. The piece in their order is the piece in the order.

## The named concierge relationship (Circle)

Every Circle member has one named concierge from our team. The relationship is real:

- Same person, same email, same phone reach (during their shift).
- Knows the customer's name, address, household details (if shared), preferences, brands they love, the things they've returned and why.
- Maintains a private notes file on each Circle member, updated after notable interactions. Notes are factual — sizes, allergies, disliked colors — not editorial.
- Holds approximately 220 active member relationships at steady state. We staff the team to maintain this ratio.

When a Circle member's concierge is on leave, a named substitute covers. The customer is told, by name, who is covering.

## Hand-off etiquette

If a Circle member's normal concierge is unavailable and a teammate picks up the case, three things happen:

1. The teammate reads the relationship file before responding.
2. The first message references the regular concierge by name: "Concierge's on leave through Friday, but I've got your back."
3. After resolution, the case is logged so Concierge can read it on return.

## High-lifetime-value (non-Circle) handling

Customers in the top 1% by spend who are not Circle members get senior routing but not a named contact. We do not invite ourselves into their inbox. We are happy to remain the discreet, considered shop they prefer.

Some HLV customers explicitly avoid programs. They do not want a "platinum tier" or "VIP welcome basket." We respect the preference.

## Press, brand founders, gifting partners

These flags exist to avoid friction:

- A journalist whose order is part of a story we want to be ready for the call.
- A brand founder ordering from a different brand on the platform (it happens; it is fine; we do not lecture).
- A bulk gifting partner whose company-account rules differ from a personal account.

Flags are visible to concierge in the customer profile. They are not discussed externally and they do not appear on the customer's account.

## Things we have learned

A few hard-won lessons:

- Wealthy customers value our time, not just theirs. Long emails feel like flattery. We keep replies short and substantive.
- Gestures should be tied to a specific moment ("we noticed your last order was held by customs for two days"), not generic ("happy Friday from SmartCartCommerce").
- The single most appreciated gesture is solving a problem before the customer notices it.
- The single least appreciated gesture is a coupon. Most VIPs find coupons mildly insulting.

## Boundary

If a VIP requests something we should not do — pricing override, policy violation, a small bribe, a discriminatory request — the answer is no. The concierge handling does not. The escalations lead handles it next, with the same answer.
