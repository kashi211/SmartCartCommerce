# Refund Decision Tree

**Document type:** Internal — concierge
**Owner:** Saket Panwar
**Last reviewed:** 16 March 2026

---

## Purpose

This is the practical decision flow concierge uses when a customer is requesting a refund. It is not a script. It is a structured way to think.

The default at every branch: when in doubt, lean toward the customer.

---

## Step 1 — Why are they returning?

Listen for the actual reason. Often the reason in the first sentence is not the real reason. Acknowledge what they said before deciding what to do.

Categorize roughly into:

- **Product issue** — damaged, defective, not as described.
- **Fit / sizing** — doesn't fit, wrong size ordered.
- **Change of mind** — no longer wanted; gift not landing; bought duplicate.
- **Service issue** — late, wrong item, missing piece.
- **External life event** — moving, financial change, recipient deceased, relationship change.

The category drives the right resolution, not the answer.

---

## Step 2 — Is the standard policy enough?

For most cases, the answer is yes. The 60-day window, free return shipping in home markets, fast refund timing — these handle the majority. Use them.

If the case is within standard policy:

- Send the return label.
- Offer exchange where useful.
- Confirm refund timing.
- Move on.

Don't over-explain. Don't apologize repeatedly. The customer asked for a return; we are giving one. Be warm and brief.

---

## Step 3 — Outside standard policy?

If outside policy, walk down this branch:

### Was it our service that failed?

If our shipping was late, our packaging caused damage, our communication misled the customer, or we surprised them with a charge — yes, refund. Don't cite policy; we are responsible.

### Is it a Care Promise issue?

Manufacturing defect, structural failure, dye instability, hardware breakage within the coverage window — yes, refund or replacement, customer's choice. Don't require photos beyond what's reasonable to confirm.

### Is it within the 60-day window with a normal reason?

Yes — refund. The standard policy applies. We don't argue eligibility on a return label being applied 47 days vs. 53 days after delivery.

### Is it past 60 days, with a normal reason?

This is the gray zone.

- For Circle members: yes, refund. The relationship is the answer.
- For HLV non-Circle: probably yes, especially within 75-90 days.
- For new or low-history customers: case by case. The reason matters. "I forgot" past 60 days: probably not. "I was hospitalized and the bag has been in my hallway": yes.

Default to yes when the situation is sympathetic.

### External life event?

Always yes. The economics of returning a $200 candle when someone has just lost a parent are not the economics that matter. We refund. We send a brief, warm note.

### Pattern of abuse?

Real, but rare. Indicators: 80%+ return rate, returning items in worn condition, returning empty packages, returns of brand-specific items that resell on grey-market sites within days.

If the pattern is suspected, do NOT confront in the moment. Process the current return at policy. Flag the account internally for review by the abuse team.

---

## Step 4 — How do we deliver the answer?

A refund is the answer; how we say it matters.

**Good (warm, short):**
> Hi (customer), all set — the kettle is on its way back to us. I've issued the refund to your card; it should land in 3-5 business days. If the second one isn't right either, we've got more options to try. Let me know.

**Bad (defensive, long):**
> Dear customer, thank you for reaching out to SmartCartCommerce. As per our policy, returns are accepted within 60 days of delivery in original condition. We have reviewed your request and are pleased to confirm we are able to process it as a courtesy refund on this occasion…

The bad version uses "as a courtesy" — implying we are doing them a favor — and "as per our policy" — citing rule when we should be citing care. Neither belongs in our voice.

---

## Step 5 — Goodwill on top?

Sometimes a small additional gesture is right. Sometimes it's not.

**Right:**
- Service-side error: $20-50 credit toward next purchase, applied automatically.
- Item delayed > 7 days past estimate: $20 credit.
- A second-time issue with the same brand: a $50 credit, plus a brief escalation note to the brand.

**Wrong:**
- A coupon on every refund (it makes the refund feel transactional).
- A goodwill credit that requires the customer to use it within 30 days (cheap).
- A goodwill credit attached to a survey ("rate us 5 stars").

When in doubt, no add-on. The honest, fast refund is the gesture.

---

## Step 6 — Document

Every refund is logged with reason category. We use this data quarterly to surface patterns: brands with rising return rates, listings with sizing drift, fulfillment centers with damage spikes.

Concierge is not asked to score the customer. We do not maintain refund-frequency tags on customer profiles for service-quality purposes.
