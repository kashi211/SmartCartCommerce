# A Letter from the Founder

**Document type:** Public correspondence
**Author:** Saket Panwar
**Originally published:** September 2019. Lightly edited September 2024.

---

Dear reader,

I started SmartCartCommerce because of a teapot.

In the spring of 2018, on a long detour through the Hudson Valley, I walked into a small ceramics studio outside Hudson, New York. The studio belonged to a ceramicist who had been making the same tulip-shaped teapot, by hand, for thirty-one years. She was making about forty a year. She had no website. Her son had built her an Instagram account that she did not check. The teapot was the most beautiful object I had seen in months. It cost $68. She told me, almost apologetically, that she was thinking of stopping. There was no market she could find.

I bought the teapot. I brought it home. And by the end of that weekend, I had landed on the conclusion that has shaped everything since: the problem was not her. The problem was that there was no place — no real, trustworthy, *findable* place — where her teapot and the people who would love her teapot could meet.

The mass marketplaces could find her teapot, in the sense that her listing could be uploaded to them. But the listing would drown. It would be optimized into something it wasn't. It would be priced against teapots that were not teapots in the same sense. The customer who needed her teapot would never find it, because the algorithm was not built to help that customer; it was built to maximize gross merchandise value across a hundred million SKUs.

I thought there had to be a different shape of business. Something smaller. Something where curation was the core asset, not a marketing word. Something where every brand felt represented, not aggregated. Something that was, frankly, a place rather than a platform.

I named it SmartCartCommerce. The name is plain on purpose. *Smart*, because the work the platform does on the customer's behalf — the curation, the editorial, the concierge — is meant to make a complicated set of decisions feel uncomplicated. *Cart*, because I am unashamed about being a place where things are bought; I did not want a name that pretended otherwise. *Commerce*, because that is the honest word for what passes between a maker and the person who chooses their work. I considered something more poetic. I chose clarity instead.

Seven years in, we have around 380 brands. The ceramicist from Hudson is one of them. Her son now manages her listings. She is making about ninety teapots a year, which is a lot for her. We send her flowers on her birthday. She sends us a postcard every Christmas.

I have been told a hundred times that we should grow faster. We have raised three rounds of capital from investors who agreed to our pace before they wired the money. We grow at the speed our curation allows, and our curation is allowed to be slow because slowness is, in our case, the entire product.

If you are reading this because you bought something from us, thank you. If you are reading this because you are considering joining us as a brand, please understand that we will not be a fast yes, and that this is the most honest thing I can tell you about how we work. If you are reading this because you are evaluating us as an employer, please understand that the values in this letter are not aspirational — they are the operating manual.

The teapot, by the way, sits on my desk in our Tribeca studio. It still pours beautifully. I use it almost every day.

With care,

Saket
