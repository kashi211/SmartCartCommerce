# Brand Voice & Tone Guidelines

**Document type:** Editorial standard
**Owner:** Saket Panwar
**Last reviewed:** 02 February 2026
**Applies to:** All customer-facing copy, including product descriptions, emails, support responses, app notifications, and packaging inserts.

---

## The voice in one line

A knowledgeable friend, with a strong point of view, writing for an intelligent reader who is short on time.

## The four pillars

### Considered

We never write the first thing that comes to mind. Every sentence is a choice. We prefer specificity to generality, the concrete noun to the abstract one. *"A linen the color of dry hay"* is better than *"a versatile neutral linen."*

### Restrained

We are not enthusiastic on the customer's behalf. We do not use the words *amazing*, *incredible*, *must-have*, *game-changer*, or *unlock*. We do not use exclamation marks except when quoting someone. We trust the reader to recognize quality without being told.

### Plainspoken

We use ordinary words. We write at roughly an eighth-grade reading level. We avoid trade jargon unless the audience is the trade. *"Hand-finished"* is fine. *"Artisanally crafted"* is not.

### Warm

We are not cold. Restraint is not the same as distance. We notice. We acknowledge effort. We use contractions. We respond to a customer's bad day like a person, not like a brand.

## Voice characteristics, with examples

**We say:** "This kettle is heavy. It is meant to live on the stove, not in a cupboard."
**Not:** "Our premium kettle is designed for the discerning home chef who values quality and craftsmanship."

**We say:** "The dye is plant-based, which means the color will shift over time. Most customers find this charming. Some do not. It's worth knowing before you buy."
**Not:** "Naturally dyed and built to last forever!"

**We say:** "We held your order an extra day to consolidate it with the second piece. Apologies if you were watching the tracker."
**Not:** "Your order is on its way! Track here."

## Tone shifts by context

The voice is constant. The tone modulates.

| Context | Tone shift |
|---|---|
| Product description | Curatorial, informative, slightly formal |
| Order confirmation | Warm, plainspoken, brief |
| Support response (general) | Warm, attentive, problem-first |
| Support response (apology) | Direct, accountable, no hedging |
| Marketing email | Editorial, lightly newsy |
| Push notification | Quiet, never urgent |
| Error message | Specific, kind, fixable |

## Words we avoid

Curated (we are; we do not need to say it), authentic, journey, experience (as a verb), elevate, indulge, treat yourself, unlock, exclusive, premium, luxe, drop, stock up, get yours, hurry, last chance, today only, decorative emoji at end of sentences.

## Words we use carefully

*Beautiful* — only when it is. *Limited* — only when there is a real edition number. *Handmade* — only when each step is. *Sustainable* — only with a specific claim attached.

## A note on capitalization

Sentence case for everything: headers, buttons, navigation, section titles. Title Case feels like a brochure.

## A note on punctuation

We use the Oxford comma. We use em dashes generously. We do not use emoji in any customer-facing or internal communication; this is a professional platform.
