# Curation Standards

**Document type:** Operating standard
**Owner:** Saket Panwar
**Last reviewed:** 28 January 2026
**Audience:** Internal curators, brand partnerships team, leadership.

---

## Why we curate

Most marketplaces optimize for inventory breadth. We optimize for the customer's confidence that anything they buy from us is worth buying. The two strategies are not compatible. We are explicit about which one we run.

Curation is the single most important thing SmartCartCommerce does. Every other system on the platform — the editorial layer, the concierge, the membership program, the take-back program — exists to support a customer's confidence in the curation. If the curation fails, nothing downstream matters.

## The seven gates

Every brand that joins SmartCartCommerce passes through seven gates. They are non-negotiable. A brand can be perfect on six and fail one and we will not list them.

### Gate 1 — The maker is identifiable

We must be able to name the person, family, or studio responsible for the work. "A factory in Italy" is not enough. We need a name, a location, and a way to verify both.

### Gate 2 — The product is made, not assembled

At least one transformative step in the product's creation must be performed by hand or by craft equipment under direct human supervision. Pure assembly of pre-made components disqualifies the brand.

### Gate 3 — The materials are honest

The materials in the product match the description and the price. Vegan leather is described as such; "leather-look" is rejected. Plated metal is plated, not "gold." Single-origin claims are documented.

### Gate 4 — The pricing is defensible

We require brands to provide a brief cost breakdown (materials, labor, overhead, margin). We are not policing margin — many of our brands deserve more than they take. We are confirming the price is not arbitrary and that the maker is paid.

### Gate 5 — The story is real

Every brand has a story. We require it to be true. We will fact-check claims about origin, founding date, founders' backgrounds, and any award or press citation.

### Gate 6 — The labor is paid

We require a signed declaration that all labor in the supply chain is fairly compensated. For brands with employees, this means a wage at or above local prevailing wage for the craft. For independent makers, this means the maker is the primary economic beneficiary.

### Gate 7 — There is something here we don't have

The product fills a gap on the platform. We will reject a wonderful brand if its work duplicates a brand we already carry. We do not run a contest of similar brands.

## How a brand gets in

Brands do not apply through a public form. We discover brands four ways: scout-led (our curators travel and visit), nominated (existing brands or trusted retailers introduce us), competition-led (we run two open calls per year, one for emerging makers and one for international makers), and inherited (we acquired a small competitor in 2023 and onboarded their roster after re-curating).

The full process from first contact to first listing typically takes 90–180 days. We will not rush it.

## How a brand stays in

Curation is not a one-time event. Every brand is reviewed annually against the seven gates plus performance criteria (customer satisfaction, dispute rate, fulfillment reliability). A brand can be quietly delisted at any time. We do not publicize delistings; we do not believe in shaming small businesses.

We have delisted 31 brands in the last 24 months. The most common reason is gate 6 (labor) or gate 5 (story drift after acquisition).

## What curation is not

Curation is not personal taste. Our curators do not list things they personally love at the expense of things customers love. Curation is a discipline, applied by people with a specific kind of trained eye, in service of a customer who cannot do this work themselves.
