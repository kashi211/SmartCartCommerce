# Email Newsletter Standards

**Document type:** Editorial + marketing standard
**Owner:** Saket Panwar
**Last reviewed:** 14 March 2026

---

## How we use email

We send three streams of email to subscribers:

1. **The Weekly** — our editorial newsletter, mostly Tuesdays.
2. **The Letter** — a long-form occasional dispatch from the founders, ~6 times a year.
3. **Operational emails** — order confirmations, ship notifications, returns, etc. — covered by other operational standards, not this one.

This document is about the first two.

## What we don't do

A list of practices common in e-commerce email that we have explicitly chosen not to do:

- **Daily emails.** Most platforms in our category email almost daily. We send weekly at most for the editorial stream.
- **Subject-line urgency.** No "Last chance," "Today only," "Don't miss," "Selling fast," "Final hours."
- **Subject-line emoji prefix.** Decorative emoji in subject lines is not permitted on any of our channels. Quiet, descriptive subject lines.
- **Promotional discounts in subject lines.** We don't run sales, so this doesn't arise.
- **Personalized "we miss you" win-back campaigns** that try to manufacture intimacy. The dishonesty is detectable.
- **Re-targeting "you left this in your cart" emails.** We send a single, brief reminder 24 hours after cart abandonment if the customer is signed in. After that, nothing.
- **Daily-deal carousels.** We do not have daily deals.
- **A/B-tested subject lines optimized purely for open rate.** We do test, but we test for tonal fit, not maximal opens.

## The Weekly — content

Each Tuesday's issue is roughly 350-500 words plus 4-6 images. Structure:

- A 60-word lead — what's on the editorial team's mind this week.
- One or two highlights — a new brand, a maker visit, an editorial guide. Always with a curatorial perspective.
- A small section called "On our shelves" — what people on our team are currently using, reading, or wearing. Genuine.
- A closing micro-section: an upcoming showroom event, a journal preview, a customer letter we loved.

Each issue is signed by a real person from the editorial team.

## The Letter — content

The Letter is longer (1,500-2,500 words), text-heavy, and personal. Topics have included: how we choose new categories, what we got wrong in 2024, a defense of slowness, an essay on storage as a craft, a meditation on returning things.

It is sent to all newsletter subscribers, not just Circle members. About 32% of recipients read it through.

## Cadence

We do not increase cadence around peak shopping seasons. We will sometimes skip a week if the editorial team doesn't have something worth sending. Subscribers tell us this is unusual and welcome.

## Subscriber management

Subscribers join through:

- Account creation (opt-in, off by default).
- Footer signup on the website (clear).
- Showroom in-person signup with explicit consent.

We do not buy lists. We do not import contact databases. We do not subscribe customers to the newsletter just because they made a purchase.

Unsubscribe is one click in any email. After unsubscribe, we send transactional only. We do not retain past-subscriber emails for re-engagement campaigns.

## Personalization

Light touch. We segment for:

- Region (so EU subscribers don't see a US-only event).
- Circle membership (Circle-specific items get a small additional section).
- Stated preferences (a subscriber who has indicated "no fragrance" doesn't get fragrance highlights).

We do NOT personalize based on:

- Browsing behavior on third-party sites.
- Cart contents (beyond the single cart-reminder mentioned above).
- Predictive "next-best item" models.
- Demographic inference.

## Design

Plain, image-first, single-column. Text large enough to read on a phone without zooming. No "view in browser" exhortation; no "trouble seeing this email?" boilerplate. The HTML is readable as plain text in any client that can't render fully.

We test rendering across Apple Mail, Gmail (web + iOS + Android), Outlook (web + desktop), Yahoo, Fastmail, ProtonMail.

## Subject lines

Descriptive, declarative, lowercase: "the brass in edinburgh," "what we're reading this week," "a quiet new candle from kyoto." We violate Title Case here on purpose; it's part of the brand restraint.

## Metrics we watch

- Open rate (hovers around 38-44%, well above category benchmark).
- Genuine engagement (clicks, but on editorial pages, not just product pages).
- Unsubscribe rate (we want this low, but we are not afraid of it; better unsubscribed than annoyed).
- Reply rate (we encourage replies; the team reads them).

We do not chase open rate at the expense of voice.

## Reviewed annually

Standards reviewed each January, with input from editorial and customer-experience teams. Subscribers occasionally write to us with feedback on the newsletter; we read all of it.
