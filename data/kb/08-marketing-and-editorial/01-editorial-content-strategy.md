# Editorial Content Strategy

**Document type:** Editorial standard
**Owner:** Saket Panwar
**Last reviewed:** 06 March 2026

---

## What we make

SmartCartCommerce's editorial team produces five surfaces of original content. They share a voice (see *Brand Voice & Tone Guidelines*) but serve different purposes.

1. **Product copy** — the description, materials, fit, and origin notes on every product page. The most-read editorial we produce.
2. **Brand profiles** — long-form features on a maker, with photography commissioned by us, published quarterly in the journal and selectively on the website.
3. **Editorial guides** — themed gift and discovery guides ("Things made of brass," "What to send a friend in March," "A small wardrobe").
4. **The Quarterly Journal** — physical magazine sent to Circle members. ~80 pages, mostly original.
5. **The newsletter** — a weekly editorial dispatch sent to subscribers. Short. (See *Email Newsletter Standards*.)

We do not run a blog as such. We do not produce SEO-driven listicles. We do not republish brand-supplied marketing copy under our masthead.

## What we don't write

A specific list, because the absence is the content strategy:

- "Top 10" listicles.
- Trend pieces tied to a season or a celebrity.
- "How to wear" instructional pieces with a strong "you should buy" framing.
- Comparative reviews against external products.
- Sponsored or "in partnership with" content.
- Aggregator-style summaries ("here's what's new this week").

The reason: each of these would degrade the editorial layer that makes the platform useful.

## Voice in editorial specifically

The voice doc covers the general principles. For editorial:

- **Long form is permitted.** A brand profile can be 1,800-2,400 words. A product description should not be.
- **Curatorial perspective is required.** Every piece has a point of view. "Here is a thing" is not editorial; it's a description.
- **Names matter.** We name the makers. We name the materials. We name the place. Generic abstractions ("a craftsman in Italy") are not allowed.
- **Numbers, where they belong.** The number of pieces in an edition. The years a maker has been at it. The temperature a kiln runs. We use specifics to ground the reader.

## Editorial process

A piece moves through:

1. **Brief** — written by the curator or commissioned writer, 200-300 words, agreed with the editorial director.
2. **Reporting** — interviews, studio visits, materials research. Documented in a private notes file.
3. **Draft 1** — by the writer.
4. **Edit 1** — by the editorial director or a senior editor.
5. **Fact check** — claims verified, attributions confirmed, numbers double-checked.
6. **Brand sign-off where applicable** — the brand confirms the maker name, founding year, and any fact about their operation. Not a copy approval — they cannot rewrite our framing.
7. **Final read + publish.**

Average time from brief to publish: 4-7 weeks for a brand profile, 1-2 days for a guide.

## Photography

Editorial photography is commissioned and owned by SmartCartCommerce (see *Photography & Listing Standards*). We work with a stable of about 14 photographers across our four headquarters cities. Style: natural light, subject-respectful framing, no over-saturation, no models posing.

Editorial photography licensing back to brands is permitted for non-commercial use; commercial use (paid advertising, retail buyer presentations) requires separate negotiation.

## What we measure

Editorial is held to different metrics than commerce surfaces:

- Time spent reading (engaged time, not bounce-prone metrics).
- Customer-survey signal: "Do our editorial pieces help you understand what you're buying?"
- Brand satisfaction with how they were portrayed.
- Critical reception (we read what people say externally about our journal; we don't pay attention to it for content decisions, but we listen).

We do NOT measure editorial primarily by:

- Clicks to product pages (this would corrupt incentives).
- Conversion attribution (similar reason).
- Email open rate (we send sparingly).

## Independence

Editorial sits separately from brand partnerships and from marketing. The editorial director reports to the COO, not to the CMO. Editorial decisions cannot be overridden by sales or marketing leadership.

We have applied this in concrete situations:

- We delisted a brand profile in 2024 after the brand pressured us to retitle it; the profile remained available unedited until they relisted with us in 2025.
- We declined a partnership-funded series in 2023 that would have featured a single brand across multiple journal issues; the brand did not become a partner.
