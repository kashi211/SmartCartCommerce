# Influencer Partnership Guidelines

**Document type:** Marketing standard
**Owner:** Saket Panwar
**Last reviewed:** 18 February 2026

---

## How we think about influencer marketing

SmartCartCommerce does not run a transactional influencer program. We do not pay creators to post on a CPM or per-post basis. We do not send products to creators in exchange for a commitment to post. We do not ask creators to use specific hashtags or apply specific filters.

This is uncommon in our category. We made the choice deliberately. The audience we serve mostly does not respond to traditional influencer marketing. Many of them, when they detect a paid placement, react negatively to both the creator and the brand. The economics for us are also difficult: a per-post CPM model dilutes margin without measurable lift.

## What we do

### Editorial-first creator features

Our editorial team identifies a small number of writers, photographers, designers, chefs, and craftspeople whose work overlaps with our world — not "influencers" in the social-marketing sense, but practitioners whose audiences trust their taste. We profile them in our editorial channels.

A typical feature: a long-form interview, a portrait series, a "what's on her shelf" or "what's in her studio" essay. The featured person is paid for their time at standard editorial rates ($800-2,400 per feature, depending on scope). They are not paid for posting about it on their own channels.

About 70% of featured people share the piece on their channels organically. We expect nothing of them; we appreciate it when they do.

### Founders' circle

We maintain a small private circle of about 60 people who genuinely love our platform and use it regularly. They are notified of new arrivals before public listing (the same 48-hour Circle window). They are invited to studio events and openings. They sometimes write about pieces they love, on their own channels, on their own initiative.

These are not paid relationships. We do gift them items in some cases — typically when a new maker joins and we want them to experience the work — but with no expectation of coverage, and we tell them so explicitly.

### Disclosed gifting

Where we send gift items to a creator and they choose to post, we require they comply with applicable disclosure laws (FTC, ASA, and equivalents). We don't get to enforce that — they do — but we make the legal expectation clear in writing.

We never ask creators to soften or remove a disclosure label.

### Showroom events

Showroom events occasionally include creators among their guest list. We do not require attendees to post.

## What we don't do

- **Pay for posts on Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, or any platform on a per-post or per-engagement basis.**
- **Affiliate links** — we don't run an affiliate program. We have considered this several times. Our concern is twofold: it muddies the line between editorial recommendation and paid promotion in the customer's mind, and it creates an incentive structure for creators that does not align with our curatorial values.
- **Code-based discounts** — we don't run sales, so we don't run "use code FOOBAR for 10% off." This rules out a major mechanism creators expect.
- **Algorithmic placements with creator content** — we don't auto-feature creator posts on the website.

## When we say no

We get inbound from creators frequently. We say no to most. When we say no, we say so politely and quickly. We do not ghost. The reason is usually one of:

- Their audience and ours don't substantially overlap.
- Their content style doesn't match our voice (too transactional, too promotional).
- We've recently featured someone in a similar space.
- Their disclosure history has been spotty (a real concern; we check).

We don't say no because of follower count. Some of our most resonant features have been with people who have 4,000 engaged followers in a specific community.

## When we say yes (criteria)

- Genuine alignment with our values and aesthetic.
- A track record of careful, honest writing or photography.
- Audience that includes people we are trying to reach (not for ROI reasons — for fit).
- Willingness to be edited.

## Compensation transparency

We publish what we pay our editorial contributors annually in our sustainability report. The figures are:

- Brand profile feature (writer fee): $1,200-2,400.
- Editorial photography commission: $1,500-3,500 per shoot.
- Studio visit photography: $800-1,500.
- Quarterly journal essay: $800-1,800.

These rates are at the high end of what's market for boutique editorial work. We pay above market because we want the work to be good and we want the writer/photographer relationship to last.

## Brand-side influencer activity

Brands on our platform may run their own influencer programs, separate from us. We do not regulate or restrict that, but the editorial layer on SmartCartCommerce itself remains independent. A brand partnership with a creator does not entitle them to editorial coverage from us.

## Reviewed annually

This policy is reviewed each January. We revisit our positions on each item. So far we have not changed.
