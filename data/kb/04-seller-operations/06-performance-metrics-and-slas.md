# Performance Metrics & SLAs

**Document type:** Brand-facing operational standard
**Owner:** Saket Panwar
**Last reviewed:** 21 February 2026

---

## Why we measure

We measure to surface real problems, not to gamify behavior. The goal of the brand scorecard is to tell a brand and its account lead what is going well, what is drifting, and what needs attention. Numbers below thresholds trigger conversation, not auto-deactivation.

## The five metrics that matter

### 1. Order-to-ship time

Time from order placement to carrier handoff, measured in business hours.

| Brand commitment | Standard | Critical threshold |
|---|---|---|
| 24h | ≤ 24h average | > 36h average over 30 days |
| 48h | ≤ 48h average | > 72h average over 30 days |
| 72h | ≤ 72h average | > 96h average over 30 days |
| Made-to-order | Per stated lead time | Slip > 7 days on any single order |

Critical threshold breach: account lead conversation within 5 business days.

### 2. On-time delivery rate

Percentage of orders that arrive within the customer-facing estimated window. We measure carrier-confirmed delivery date against the customer's promised date.

- Standard: ≥ 92%
- Critical threshold: < 86% over 30 days

### 3. Damage rate

Percentage of orders generating a damage report. Reported by the customer, validated by concierge.

- Standard: ≤ 1.0%
- Critical threshold: > 1.5% over 90 days

A damage rate above 1.5% triggers a quality investigation jointly led by your account lead and our quality team. Common causes: under-packaging (most common), changed material supplier, transit handling. We work it through with you.

### 4. Customer satisfaction (CSAT)

A 1-5 satisfaction question on every order, sent 7 days after delivery.

- Standard: ≥ 4.4
- Critical threshold: < 4.1 over 90 days

CSAT is segmented by reason: product quality, fit, packaging, shipping, communication. We share the segmentation with you so you can act on the real signal.

### 5. Return rate

Percentage of items returned, measured against category-adjusted benchmarks.

- Apparel benchmarks: 12–18% normal, > 22% needs attention
- Home benchmarks: 4–8% normal, > 12% needs attention
- Beauty benchmarks: 2–4% normal, > 6% needs attention
- Food benchmarks: < 1% normal, > 2% always investigated
- Jewelry benchmarks: 6–10% normal, > 14% needs attention

High return rate above the upper bound triggers a fit/quality conversation. We do not penalize the brand financially for legitimate returns.

## What we don't measure (but you might expect us to)

We do not measure:

- Click-through rate on listings.
- Add-to-cart conversion.
- Time-on-page.
- Promotional uplift.

We do not surface these because they do not reflect quality of work — they reflect surface decisions our editorial team makes about merchandising and platform design. Holding a brand to those would shift accountability inappropriately.

## Quarterly business review

Every brand's account lead schedules a QBR each quarter. The QBR covers the five metrics, customer feedback themes, upcoming launches, inventory health, and any operational concerns. QBRs are conversations, not interrogations. We aim to leave each session with a small set of joint commitments for the following quarter.

## Incident-level SLAs

For specific issues:

| Incident | Brand response SLA |
|---|---|
| Concierge ticket about a brand-specific issue | 4 business hours during business days |
| Quality investigation initiated by SmartCartCommerce | 48 hours to first written response |
| Stockout discovered post-order | 4 hours to confirm + propose resolution |
| Authenticity question (jewelry/watches) | 24 hours to confirm chain-of-custody documents |
| Recall (rare, but real) | Immediate; full cooperation expected |

We measure brand response time on incidents alongside the five core metrics.

## Performance improvement plans

For brands consistently below threshold on any metric, we move into a 90-day performance improvement period. The PIP is collaborative: we agree on root cause, a small set of changes, and a check-in cadence. Approximately 70% of brands who enter a PIP exit it successfully within the period.

## Off-boarding

A small number of brands do not respond to a PIP. Off-boarding, when it happens, is handled with care: we run down current inventory, settle outstanding payouts, return any consigned stock, and (where appropriate) provide editorial coverage of the brand on the way out so the customer relationship is honored.
